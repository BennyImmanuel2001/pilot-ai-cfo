Create a 40-second promotional video for Pilot, an AI CFO built for the PayPal AI Hackathon.

Product description:
Pilot is a chat-driven AI CFO for single-income households and gig workers. Ask it a money question in plain English ("Can I afford a $999 e-bike?") and it projects your cash flow to month end, answers Yes / Wait / No with the math behind it, rebuilds a live dashboard around the answer, and drafts a PayPal action (a sandbox order or an autopay subscription) that only goes through when you click Approve.

Visual style:
- Real footage of the Pilot web app (dark theme): 30% chat on the left, 70% generative dashboard on the right
- Fintech dashboard visual language: KPI tiles, charts, cash-flow timeline, transaction grid
- Verdict badge animation (No / Wait / Yes) as the hero moment
- Dashboard morph transitions: old widgets out, new widgets in
- Smooth zooms into the footage with soft depth-of-field framing
- Deep navy background, blue-to-violet gradient accents, Geist typography
- Professional, calm, confident; no stock imagery

Video structure:
0–5 sec:
Problem:
"Can I actually afford this?"
Floating bill chips (rent, electric, phone) and "Payday in 23 days" drift around the question.

5–10 sec:
Introduce:
"Meet Pilot. Your AI CFO."
Pilot glyph reveal, then the app slides in.

10–20 sec:
Animate:
Ask "Can I afford a $999 e-bike?" → verdict "No" with the reasoning → cash-flow timeline dips under the safety line.
Callout: "A real decision. With the math."

20–28 sec:
Animate:
Ask about food spending, then next month → the dashboard reshapes itself each time.
Callout: "A dashboard that rebuilds around every question."

28–34 sec:
Animate:
Pilot spots a recurring bill → proposes PayPal autopay → click Approve → subscription created.
Callout: "Nothing moves without your approval."

34–37 sec:
Show:
"Ask. Decide. Act."

37–40 sec:
Show:
"Pilot"
"The AI CFO that decides, then acts."
"Powered by PayPal + AI"

End with:
"Try Pilot"

16:9 format, 1920×1080, 30 fps.
Professional SaaS advertisement.
No voiceover and no music for the first version (hackathon rules: no third-party trademarks or copyrighted music; all merchants and products shown are fictional).
